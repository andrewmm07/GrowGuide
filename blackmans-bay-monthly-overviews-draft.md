# Blackmans Bay, Tasmania (7052) — Monthly Growing Guide

## JANUARY
Summer's in full swing. Tomatoes, capsicums, eggplants, cucumbers, beans are all producing hard. Plant seedlings now if you've raised them—late capsicums and eggplants can still mature. Sow winter crops while soil's warm: carrots, beetroot, parsnips, kale, broccoli, cabbage, silverbeet, turnips. Chill lettuce seed first; cold germinates it well. This dual-season month builds autumn while protecting summer. Water deep in the morning, not shallow all day. Heat stress makes plants weak and invites pests. Mulch to hold moisture and keep roots cool.

## FEBRUARY
Still warm and productive. Plant out brassica seedlings (broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower, kale) that you started in January. Direct-sow more winter crops: carrot, cabbage, cauliflower, silverbeet, beetroot, parsnip. Lettuces and Asian greens sown now will be vigorous by autumn. Harvest summer crops regularly. Keep watering deeply—this month can bring sudden heat spikes. You're essentially closing out summer while autumn seedlings establish. By month's end, the temperature drift becomes noticeable.

## MARCH
Autumn's arrived. First frosts possible late month, especially on clear nights. Plant garlic cloves and final seedlings of celery, kale, lettuce, silverbeet. Sow beetroot, carrot, parsnip early in the month only. Then shift to cool-season crops: spinach, broad beans, peas, Asian greens. Mulch beds before frosts. Watch for bolting in brief warm spells. Growth slows visibly now. Frost protection becomes real work—fleece or cloches for tender seedlings. The shift from spring-like warmth to autumn chill is fast here.

## APRIL
Main autumn planting. Soil's cooling fast. Plant seedlings of brassicas, leeks, lettuce, silverbeet, onions. Sow spinach, broad beans, peas, Asian cabbages. Mulch heavily before hard frosts arrive. Early potatoes in frost-free pockets can still go in. Germination slows in cold soil, so be patient. Harvest remaining summer crops. The beds emptying out feel stark, but that's the point—clear space for autumn crops that'll feed you through winter. Growth is deliberate now, not frantic.

## MAY
Winter approaches. Frost is regular. Plant broad beans, garlic, leeks, spinach, leafy greens. Potatoes can go in early varieties go in if frost-free. Build soil: add compost, mulch empty beds. Growth is slow; plants are hardy by design. Harvest root crops if needed. Wet weather is typical—watch for slugs and waterlogging. The garden is settling into winter mode. It looks sparse compared to summer, but it's working hard underneath.

## JUNE
Cold month. Minimal planting now. Sow spinach and broad beans indoors in containers for later planting. Plant asparagus crowns, artichokes, more potatoes if space. Fleece or cloche anything still tender. Mulch root crops for frost protection. Soil prep matters more than planting—work in compost where you can. The garden is quiet. Use this time to plan, fix beds, build structure. Growth is slow; the plants are enduring, not thriving.

## JULY
Coldest month. Very little happens. Protect what's growing. Check mulch depth. Sow more spinach and broad beans indoors for August/September planting. Root crops are your harvest. Plan your spring garden now while you have time. Frost is deep and regular. The soil is cold and slow. This is when gardeners think and prepare rather than plant.

## AUGUST
Spring's starting. Start seedlings indoors of brassicas (cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower), lettuce, celery. Plant potatoes, asparagus, artichokes in frost-free pockets. Direct-sow peas, broad beans, spinach in warmer soil. The soil is still cold for many things—be patient. Hardened-off seedlings of cabbage, broccoli, lettuce can go out late month if frost risk is low. Growth accelerates. The light is changing noticeably.

## SEPTEMBER
Frost still possible early month, especially on clear nights. Late month is safer for seedlings. Plant hardy brassicas, lettuce, leeks, onions, root crops. Sow direct: carrots, beetroot, parsnips, cabbage, broccoli, silverbeet, peas. Start tomatoes, capsicums, eggplants indoors now under glass; they'll be ready for late October. Growth picks up. Days are warming and lengthening. This is the month where the garden's energy shifts noticeably upward.

## OCTOBER
Last serious frost risk early month. Late October is safe for tender seedlings. Plant out all seedlings: tomatoes, capsicums, eggplants, zucchini, pumpkin, squash, beans, sweetcorn. Direct-sow everything still missing: beans, peas, roots, leafy crops. Soil is warm enough now. Growth is visible week to week. The transition from autumn to spring feels complete. Planting is fast and satisfying.

## NOVEMBER
Peak planting month. Warm soil, low frost risk, long days. Plant tomatoes, capsicums, eggplants, beans, squash, pumpkin, zucchini, sweetcorn, cucumbers. Sow direct: beans, peas, squash, pumpkin, roots, leafy crops. This is the month where the garden fills up. Growth accelerates hard. Mulch heavily for summer heat to come. Watering matters; new plantings need establishing. It's busy and energising.

## DECEMBER
Full summer. Heat intensifies. Plant late summer seedlings if space allows. Direct-sow anything not yet in: beans, peas, roots, squash, pumpkin. Mulch everything—summer heat is coming and soil moisture is critical now. Water deeply in the morning before heat builds. New plantings need attention. Harvest early summer crops like peas and beans before they finish. The garden is full and productive. Growth is explosive in the warmth.
