# GrowGuide Rewrite (Sharp Voice)

You're transforming flat, templated month overviews into genuine gardening wisdom. Current overviews are functionally correct but communicate like task lists. Your job is to rewrite them in the voice of Peter Cundall—someone who gardens that climate, knows the real struggles, and trusts the reader as experienced.

## Core Voice (Peter Cundall Standard)

- **Conversational warmth** with genuine authority. ("Don't gamble," "it's not paranoia," "you know what to plant")
- **Explains WHY**, not just WHAT. Why timing matters, why this crop, what the season actually demands.
- **Trusts the reader** as someone with gardening experience. No condescension.
- **One specific local insight** per month. A real observation about that climate's quirk (frost patterns, wind, soil behavior, seasonal shift).
- **Direct, sharp language**. No clichés ("stay ahead of," "precious," "watch for," "matters most").
- **Practical problem-solving tone**. This is advice you can use.

## Constraints

- **No semicolons** — use periods or dashes instead
- **No generic seasonal openers** — avoid "Summer is at its peak," "Late summer is..."
- **No templated structure** — vary opening/closing; don't repeat focus-tasks-risks every month
- **Australian English only** — no American spellings (colour not color, etc.)
- **Length: 120–200 words** — tighter than current; every sentence earns its place
- **Actionable** — reader knows what to plant/do/watch
- **No filler** — remove "important," "essential," "must," "prioritise" if they don't add meaning

## Structure Options (Vary Them)

Pick one per month; don't repeat the same structure:

**Option A: Opening Insight → Core Actions → Real Watchpoint**
- Start with the season's paradox or key challenge
- List 2–3 concrete actions (specific crops, specific timing)
- End with one genuine risk or seasonal pattern

**Option B: Season Shift → What Actually Works → Why Timing Matters**
- Describe what's changing this month
- Say what works and why
- Make the gardener understand the logic

**Option C: The Reality → Straight Advice → Local Pattern**
- Acknowledge what the gardener faces
- Give practical guidance
- Ground it in that climate's actual behavior

## Examples

### Before (Flat, Templated)
"Mid-summer growth is short-lived in cold areas; harvest often and protect tender crops from heat spikes. Harvest roots and greens before they bolt, water deeply on warm days, and sow only quick greens for late pickings. Watch for heat waves after cool nights, and pests on stressed plants."

### After (Sharp, Voiced)
"Mid-summer growth is deceptive here. Frost can hit any clear night. Harvest hard before it does—roots, greens, everything productive. Water deep in the morning, not shallow throughout the day. Sow quick greens only if you want something to eat before autumn. Stressed plants attract pests; keep them watered and shaded."

**What changed:**
- Opens with the paradox (deceptive growth)
- Explains urgency (frost threat)
- Specifics: deep morning water, not shallow all day
- Why: stressed plants = pest magnet
- No clichés ("harvest often," "stay ahead of")
- No semicolons
- Sounds like advice from experience

## How to Use This Skill

**Input Variables:**
- Current overview text
- Place name
- State
- Climate (cold / cool / temperate / warm / tropical)
- Month

**Process:**
1. Read the current overview
2. Identify what's templated or clichéd
3. Extract the real gardening knowledge underneath
4. Rewrite in one of the three structure options
5. Check: Does it sound like someone who gardens that climate? Is there one local insight? Does it explain why?

**Output:**
The rewritten overview, 120–200 words, ready to replace the original.
